# Internal parent consultation simulation

This is an agent role-play to test the catalogue's explanation and decision flow. It is not a real patient encounter or a live-browser usability test. No prescription or measurements were invented. Suitability remained subject to an actual optometric examination and fitting assessment.

## Test scenario

Hypothetical parent of a nine-year-old; a comfortable package budget of RM2,000; clear, easy-care lenses; a hypothetical RM200 frame; both RM120 biometry follow-ups paid. The parent wanted two sensible choices and an explanation of the different clinical-study numbers.

## Initial dialogue

**Optometrist — opening**

“These lenses correct your child’s vision and are designed to help slow myopia progression. Before choosing, we check that the prescription and frame suit the lens design. We can compare the exact lens, coating and full package price side by side. Changing the coating changes everyday surface features and price; it does not create a different myopia-control design within the same lens family. What matters most to you today—keeping within a budget, light-adaptive lenses for outdoor use, or a particular replacement period? What would you like the complete package to cost?”

**Parent — priorities**

“Around RM2,000 including the frame and follow-ups would be comfortable. My child is nine, and clear, easy-clean lenses are enough. I do not want to pay more just because a number looks bigger: does 67%, 47%, 0.58 D or 0.60 D tell me which brand works best? Could you narrow this to two clear-lens choices, and tell me the complete cost with, say, a RM200 frame?”

**Optometrist — comparison and first recommendation**

“The study details matter more than which number is largest. Stellest’s 67% concerns prescription progression over two years versus single-vision lenses with at least 12 hours’ daily wear. Stellest 2.0’s about 47% concerns estimated eye-length growth over one year versus original Stellest. The iQ 0.58 D and MyoActive 0.60 D figures come from separate 12-month studies; this table does not establish which brand will work best for your child.

“With your RM200 frame and two RM120 follow-ups, RM440 is added to the lens price. Two clear-lens choices within RM2,000 are STELLEST + Crizal Rock at RM1,300, total RM1,740, and original MiYOSMART Clear + STX at RM1,300, also RM1,740. Rock’s stated scratch/smudge-resistance benefits fit your everyday-handling preference; STX is the HOYA everyday protective coating. I would start with STELLEST + Crizal Rock if our prescription and fitting checks suit it, and keep MiYOSMART Clear/STX as the other practical choice. Both are myopia-management designs; the coating is not an extra treatment level.

“The RM240 covers two biometry visits including refraction. We use the prescription and eye-length measurements to review progress. Would you like me to show the replacement terms and the exact RM1,740 summary before we proceed?”

**Parent — replacement and upgrade questions**

“RM1,740 with those items makes sense, and I like keeping this within budget. Please show the exact summary. Before I choose, if the power changes by 0.50 D after eight months, would either of these two lenses get a free replacement? Is the RM240 paying for the guarantee, or for the check-ups? I also noticed iQ has a one-year term—what would its complete clear-lens package cost, so I can decide whether the extra is worth considering?”

**Optometrist — terms, upgrade cost and handoff**

“For either of the two RM1,740 packages, the stated replacement condition is at least 0.75 D more short-sighted within six months. Your example of 0.50 D after eight months would not meet it. HOYA specifies sphere power; Stellest’s catalogue uses prescription power.

“The RM240 pays for the two check-ups—RM120 each, including refraction and eye-length measurement. The lens replacement guarantee is shown with the lens choice at no additional charge.

“MiYOSMART iQ Clear + S.P.A.R.K. is RM1,700 for the lens pair. With the same RM200 frame and RM240 follow-ups, the complete total is RM2,140: RM400 above the RM1,740 choices. Its stated guarantee is complimentary replacement if sphere power becomes at least 0.50 D more short-sighted within one year, so your eight-month example falls within that condition if the change is in sphere. The one-year period and S.P.A.R.K. handling benefits are practical reasons to consider that extra RM400; I would not use the separate study numbers as proof that it must work better for your child.

“For your stated budget, I would keep STELLEST + Crizal Rock at RM1,740 as our starting recommendation, subject to the prescription/frame checks. I’ll select that exact lens and coating, carry the RM200 frame and both RM120 visits into Review summary, and let you check the lens name, total and replacement condition before printing. You can still choose iQ at RM2,140 if those additional features are worth it to you.”

**Parent — initial acceptance**

“That is clear now. The RM240 is for the two check-ups, and the free replacement has its own power-change and time conditions. I understand iQ is RM400 above the RM1,740 choices and RM140 above my original budget. Please keep the STELLEST + Crizal Rock recommendation at RM1,740: RM1,300 lenses, RM200 frame and both RM120 visits. I would expect Use this lens to take me straight to that exact summary, with the six-month / 0.75 D replacement condition visible before printing. I did not feel pressured, and I would not need the technical design rows expanded unless I asked.”

## Moderator's focused revision request

The initial dialogue had no material clinical, arithmetic or pressure blocker. The moderator requested a short final closing that defines *sphere* in plain language and explicitly distinguishes the RM400 upgrade gap from the RM140 overrun of the RM2,000 budget, then repeats the exact summary handoff.

**Parent — response to the revision request**

“Yes, that would improve clarity. As the parent, ‘sphere’ is the one term I could still nod at without fully understanding; one plain line defining it as the main lens-power figure, separate from astigmatism, makes the guarantee meaningful. Explicitly saying iQ is RM140 above the stated budget also prevents the RM400 difference from being mistaken for the budget overrun. A brief exact RM1,740 closing would feel clearer and still unpressured.”

The revised optometrist closing and parent response are recorded below. The moderator’s rubric and scores are in moderator-review.md.

**Optometrist — revised closing**

“HOYA’s ‘sphere’ means the main plus/minus power figure on the prescription, separate from the astigmatism correction. The iQ package is RM2,140—RM140 above your RM2,000 budget and RM400 above the RM1,740 option. Your RM240 is for the two check-ups; replacement has its own power-change and time conditions.

“We’ll keep your chosen STELLEST + Crizal Rock package: RM1,300 lenses, RM200 frame and two RM120 visits, total RM1,740. Use this lens should open that exact summary, with the six-month / at-least-0.75-D prescription-power condition visible before we print.”

**Parent — revised acceptance**

“Yes—that explains the sphere term and the two different price comparisons clearly. Please use the RM1,740 STELLEST / Crizal Rock package exactly as stated. I understand what the visits cost and that replacement has separate conditions.”
