Original HOYA trade brochure

MiYOSMART iQ: 12-month results0.58 D less prescription progression and 0.27 mm less eye-length growth on average versus ordinary single-vision spectacles. Prescription progression was measured as cycloplegic spherical equivalent. Study ages 4–12. Results presented at ARVO 2026; HOYA data on file, April 2026. Individual results vary.
Understanding the two outcomesDioptres (D) describe the change in short-sighted prescription; millimetres (mm) describe how much the eye grew in length. These are different outcomes, rather than a single treatment score.
What the 9-in-10 result meansHOYA reports that 9 in 10 children aged 4–12 showed no clinically relevant myopia progression over the first 12 months of iQ wear. The brochure defines clinically relevant progression as spherical-equivalent refraction becoming at least 0.50 D more short-sighted. This threshold does not mean zero change or that every child stopped progressing. Individual results vary.
About the evidenceThe supplied HOYA brochure cites conference presentations and HOYA data on file. It states that a peer-reviewed publication will follow. Long-term D.I.M.S. platform research is background evidence and is not automatically an iQ trial.
MiYOSMART: figures from the same trial chartThe reported mean spherical-equivalent changes were −0.255 D with MiYOSMART versus −0.534 D with single vision. Axial elongation was 0.206 mm versus 0.346 mm. The comparison table shows calculated differences of 0.28 D and 0.14 mm, rounded from this chart.
Triple Enhanced Design in iQTreatment closer to the centre is intended to deliver the myopic-defocus signal to the near-peripheral retina. Higher defocus power strengthens that optical signal, and a larger zone extends treatment coverage, including for larger frames. iQ has a 6.9 mm clear zone, 41 mm treatment zone and +4.5 D defocus power; MiYOSMART has 9.4 mm, 33 mm and +3.5 D respectively. Defocus power is a treatment-design feature, not the child’s prescription.
FreeForm back surface: vision and profileiQ has an optimized aspherical back-surface design shaped using FreeForm technology, intended to reduce optical aberrations and provide a flatter lens profile. HOYA reports up to 10% lower edge thickness compared with a standard spectacle lens in its April 2026 technical data. The size of the benefit depends on the lens and prescription; it is not a promise for every pair. FreeForm is included for iQ in the brochure’s availability table.
S.P.A.R.K.: coating test resultsHOYA reports 50% greater scratch resistance and 5× greater water resistance than the previous MiYOSMART generation, based on its technical testing. The brochure references S.P.A.R.K. technical results from May 2026 and the STX MiYOSMART Green PAR assessment from June 2025. These are coating test comparisons, not additional clinical myopia-control results.
Lasting easy cleaningThe brochure describes easy-clean performance after simulated wiping equivalent to nearly three years of daily use. This is a technical durability test, not a three-year guarantee or a promise of scratch-proof lenses.
MiYOSMART: 6-month replacement guaranteeComplimentary replacement if sphere power becomes at least 0.75 D more short-sighted within 6 months (an additional −0.75 D or more).
MiYOSMART iQ: 1-year replacement guaranteeComplimentary replacement if sphere power becomes at least 0.50 D more short-sighted within 1 year (an additional −0.50 D or more).
Sphere is the replacement criterionThese replacement terms refer to sphere power. The clinical trial outcomes use cycloplegic spherical equivalent, which is a different measurement.
Prescription availabilityBoth families are 1.59 polycarbonate, cylinder up to −4.00 D. MiYOSMART Clear supports sphere 0.00 D to −13.00 D (combined limit −13.00 D); original Chameleon and Sunbird support 0.00 D to −10.00 D (combined limit −10.00 D). iQ Clear and Chameleon support 0.00 D to −13.00 D (combined limit −13.00 D), plus +0.25 D to +4.00 D sphere subject to maximum spherical equivalent +2.00 D. Availability is confirmed when ordering.
Who it is forThe iQ study included children from age four; suitability is confirmed individually by your optometrist.

Myopia-control lenses help slow progression. Individual results vary. Your optometrist will confirm suitability and monitor progress.
