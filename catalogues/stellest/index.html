About Stellest lenses

Study details and original brochures.

Original Stellest brochure ↗

What the lenses doThe prescription corrects vision. Surrounding lenslets create a region of treatment light in front of the retina, designed to help slow eye growth.
Stellest: prescription progressionThe 67% average reduction is a two-year result versus single-vision lenses for children reporting at least 12 hours of daily wear. The trial recruited children aged 8–13 in China and measured cycloplegic spherical-equivalent refraction. This is a study average, with individual variation.
Stellest 2.0: eye-length growthAbout 47% less estimated cumulative eye-length growth versus original Stellest: 0.121 mm versus 0.228 mm over one year. This was a separate Singapore crossover trial in 50 children aged 6–10, with each eye switching design after six months. It had no single-vision control group.
Understanding the two resultsPrescription progression measures changes in lens power; axial growth measures eye length in millimetres. The studies used different outcomes, comparators and durations, so their percentages must be interpreted separately.
For everyday wearAirwear 1.59 is a lightweight, impact-resistant lens material with UV protection. Coating choice adds surface benefits; it does not change the myopia-control lens design.
Local product availabilitySphere +2.00 D to −12.00 D; cylinder up to −4.00 D. Availability is distinct from study enrolment ranges and requires an optometrist’s assessment.
Optometrist references
Stellest 2.0 percentage: pending wording checkThe existing about 47% claim is retained. The earlier review suggested about 46%; the paper wording still needs confirmation before any change.
Airwear material comparisons: footnote check pendingThe existing up-to impact resistance, weight and thickness figures are retained. Confirm the exact reference material and laboratory test basis from the original brochure footnotes.
Stellest 2.0 lenslet count: source recordThe comparison retains 1,190 lenslets, or 169 more than original Stellest. The source for this exact commercial count is pending confirmation. Source status is recorded here for optometrist reference.
Stellest 2.0: study limits and geometryEye length was the primary endpoint. Exploratory prescription differences were not significant within either phase; refraction was non-cycloplegic. Both trial designs had 11 rings. Commercial Stellest 2.0 has 12 rings to support a wider diameter and broader frame choice; the added ring was not the tested variable. The crossover had no washout period.
H.A.L.T. MAX optical designIncreased lenslet power and asphericity produce a deeper treatment-light region farther in front of the retina. The manufacturer’s “twice the signal” refers to treatment-volume depth, not doubled prescription power or doubled clinical efficacy.
U.S. practitioner instructionsThe supplied U.S. IFU recommends at least 10 hours a day, six days a week. This regional guidance is separate from the local brochure’s ≥12-hour study subgroup and must not be used as the wearing condition for its 67% claim.
