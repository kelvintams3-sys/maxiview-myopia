About ZEISS MyoActive

Clinical evidence and lens details.

Original ZEISS brochure ↗

What the study foundOn average over 12 months, children wearing MyoActive had 0.60 D less prescription progression and 0.22 mm less eye-length growth than children wearing ordinary single-vision lenses. Prescription progression was measured as cycloplegic spherical equivalent; eye-length growth was measured separately.
About the evidenceFirst-year results from an ongoing two-year randomized, double-masked, multicentre trial in China (NCT06563700). Study ages 7–13 years; 56 children in the MyoActive group and 56 in the single-vision group. ZEISS data on file, July 2026; submitted for publication. These are not completed two-year results.
Two M.O.V.E. design innovationsA 7 mm clear central zone is surrounded by small cylindrical treatment elements with different positive powers, from +1.50 D to +5.50 D. Their powers are deliberately non-aligned, so gaze paths do not repeatedly encounter the same sequence of powers. Distance-correction regions remain between the treatment circles. These are element powers, not the patient’s prescription.
Why the changing pattern mattersDynamic Signal Theory aims to keep the treatment signal variable as the eyes move, reducing repetitive stimulation. Eye-tracking and optical modelling support the intended changing signal. They do not establish that preventing adaptation caused the clinical effect, or that other lens designs stop working.
ClearFocus has a different roleThe optimized back surface is designed to minimize peripheral hyperopic defocus while supporting the prescription correction and intended myopic treatment defocus across gaze directions. This is a back-surface optical feature, separate from the clear central zone.
Adaptation and visual performance98% of children in the study adapted within one week. Central high- and low-contrast acuity were maintained. When looking through the treatment zones, peripheral acuity was approximately 3–4 letters lower than with single-vision lenses. ClearFocus does not mean equal clarity through every part of the lens.
Prescription availability1.59 polycarbonate with UVProtect. Sphere 0.00 D to −9.00 D; cylinder up to −6.00 D; combined prescription up to −9.00 D. Maximum diameter 75 mm, subject to combined prescription. Availability is checked when ordering.
1-year replacement guaranteeComplimentary replacement if prescription power becomes at least 0.50 D more short-sighted within 1 year (an additional −0.50 D or more).
Optometrist study details & flagged brochure claims
Platinum UV price: pending confirmationThe supplied brochure lists RM1,600; this catalogue retains its approved RM1,800 price. Confirm the intended retail price before changing it.
Observed study wear and safetyStudy wear averaged approximately 12 hours per day; this was observed wear, not a prescribed minimum. No lens-related adverse events were reported over the first 12 months.
ZEISS efficacy positioning · flagged for reviewThe brochure describes MyoActive as ZEISS’s most effective myopia-management lens to date and uses the phrase “highest-ever average efficacy”. These are ZEISS’s claims. Their comparative scope needs confirmation: the results presented here compare MyoActive with single-vision lenses and do not establish a ranking against MiYOSMART iQ or Stellest.
ZEISS “100%” eye-growth claim · flagged for reviewThe brochure states that 100% of children have emmetropic-like eye growth. The white paper discusses comparison with age-matched emmetropic reference growth; this was not a concurrent randomized emmetropic control group. How the brochure’s 100% wording maps to individual children needs clarification. The claim does not mean that every child’s eye stopped growing.
Group means and relative reductions · 12 monthsSpherical-equivalent change averaged −0.23 D with MyoActive versus −0.83 D with single vision: a 0.60 D difference and 72% relative reduction. Axial elongation averaged 0.11 mm versus 0.33 mm: a 0.22 mm difference and 67% relative reduction.
Interpreting the mean prescription changeThe −0.23 D MyoActive group mean was smaller than the usual 0.25 D subjective prescription step. This does not mean that progression was zero or that no child progressed.
First-year intervals and age subgroupsSpherical-equivalent progression was reduced by 74% during months 0–6 and 69% during months 6–12 versus single vision. This covers the first year and does not establish indefinite maintained efficacy. For ages 7–9, between-group differences were 0.69 D and 0.27 mm; for ages 10–13, 0.52 D and 0.18 mm. These are subgroup results, separate from the full-study averages.
Comparison with reference eye growthReported axial elongation was below age-matched emmetropic reference growth across the studied ages. The reference was not a concurrent randomized emmetropic control group. It does not mean every child’s eye will stop growing or follow a normal-growth trajectory.
Observed axial shorteningShortening from baseline was reported in 47.3% of MyoActive eyes at 3 months and 30.0% at 12 months. Among eyes showing shortening, the mean at 3 months was −0.08 ± 0.07 mm. This is an observed subgroup finding, not proof of a cure or established reversal of myopia.
Children’s reported experienceNear, intermediate and distance ratings remained high. At 12 months, all study children reported liking the lenses; 85.7% liked them very much. Separately, the brochure reports that at dispensing, 93% rated distance vision “very good” and 93% rated near vision “good” or “very good”. These time points and questions differ.

Myopia-control lenses help slow progression. Individual results vary. Your optometrist will assess suitability and monitor prescription and eye length.
